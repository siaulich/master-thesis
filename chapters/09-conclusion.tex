\chapter{Conclusion}
\label{chap:conclusion}
\section{Summary}
\label{sec:summary}

Dileptonic decays of top quarks enable the exploration of numerous open questions and facilitate precise measurements. Although this channel exhibits a low branching ratio, it offers a particularly clean event environment.

Recent studies of quantum and quasi-bound-state effects near the production threshold have motivated further investigation of this region. Dileptonic decays offer unique precision for probing spin structures in the underlying physical processes.

Despite the clean environment provided by this channel, several challenges persist. The presence of two neutrinos from the leptonic decay of the $W$-bosons results in six unknown momentum components. Additionally, the selection and assignment of $b$-jets to their parent top quarks introduce further ambiguities. While neutrino regression has been previously investigated, neutrino assignment remains understudied, and both tasks are inherently interconnected. Accurate measurement of variables related to the internal structure requires full reconstruction of the top quark four-momenta. Therefore, reconstruction quality is critical to the sensitivity of top-quark pair studies.

A transformer-based machine-learning model was developed and trained to address the jet assignment problem. Various architectures and layouts were evaluated, all demonstrating similar performance despite incorporating different kinematic symmetries. The resulting model substantially improved jet assignment efficiency. This enhanced accuracy led to reduced reconstruction errors and improved resolution of key variables, thereby increasing the sensitivity of subsequent measurements.

Beyond the overall improvements, detailed analysis identified specific regions of phase space that benefit most from machine-learning-based reconstruction.

The region near the production threshold was particularly challenging. In this regime, no significant improvement in accuracy over baseline methods was observed. Certain kinematic features contributing to reconstruction difficulties were identified, although some effects remain unexplained.

To further enhance event reconstruction and address the interdependencies between jet assignment and neutrino regression, a model capable of performing both tasks was developed. The architecture extended the previous design by adding an output head for neutrino momenta. Although resolving the interdependency was an initial objective, the combined approach yielded limited improvements. Comparisons between single-task and multi-task models revealed no enhancement in jet-assignment accuracy and only minor gains in regression.

However, some cross-talk between the tasks was observed.

Therefore, existing tools for neutrino regression, whether analytical or machine-learning-based, are expected to provide valuable information for jet assignment.

Additionally, the developed methods can reduce errors in neutrino regression and improve the resolution of derived variables, such as spin-sensitive markers and the invariant mass of the \ttbar system. Despite these improvements, the model exhibited significant biases in the reconstructed variables, resulting in distortions in their distributions. Some corrective measures were identified and discussed. The primary cause of these effects is likely the probabilistic nature of the neutrino momenta. Because the neutrinos are inherently underdetermined, they are best represented by a conditional probability density. Training a regression model collapses the output to the mean of the underlying distribution, which can introduce bias when computing nonlinear functions.

This issue can be mitigated by modeling and propagating the conditional density from the neutrino momenta to the reconstructed variables and averaging over their distributions. In general, this approach provides a more comprehensive representation of the kinematics.

The performance of the developed methods was demonstrated by reproducing the analysis of bound-state effects at the production threshold in a simplified setting. An Asimov-fit, designed to assess the potential analytical impact, indicated that uncertainties associated with the signal strength are significantly reduced by methods with enhanced resolution, such as $\nu^2$-Flows and the MultiModalTransformer. The MultiModalTransformer, in particular, achieved a threefold increase in the significance of the signal strength, representing a substantial improvement. However, in practical applications, this enhancement will be constrained by systematic uncertainties. Furthermore, the improved reconstruction provided by the MultiModalTransformer presents a notable limitation:

Reconstructed variables of the top quarks, such as the invariant mass of the \ttbar system and the spin-sensitive \chel and \chan, exhibit significant biases and distortions in their shapes. Although it has been demonstrated that this does not affect results when both the data and the simulation are similarly distorted, this issue can be critical for analyses that extract information directly from the distributions. For example, the measurement of quantum effects in Reference \cite{atlas_entanglement} utilised the slope of the \chel distribution as a marker for entanglement. Therefore, additional consideration is required for such analyses. Notably, the distributions of reconstructed masses indicate that this machine-learning-based method yields conceptually distinct results compared with other approaches and more consistently preserves physical constraints.

Therefore, it is necessary to determine which method yields the most reliable results for a given analysis and to adapt the approach accordingly.

For reconstructed variables, it is essential that they are accurately modeled by simulations. To ensure consistency in the reconstruction provided by the MultiModalTransformer, distributions of reconstructed variables, such as the top-quark mass, were compared between simulation and data collected by \atlas.

Generally, a very good agreement in the distributions was found.

This demonstrates that these tools are applicable to both simulation and event data. The distributions indicate the method's robustness for use with data and in future analyses.

Combined with the thorough evaluation of the method's performance, these findings provide strong motivation to adopt the approach developed here or similar methods in future research. Additionally, integrating two reconstruction tasks into a single model not only addresses interdependencies but also reduces computational demands. The transition to the HL-LHC, which will increase the number of events by up to an order of magnitude, will introduce further challenges related to analysis computation time. Therefore, tools that combine tasks are increasingly important as they minimize redundancies.

\section{Outlook}
\label{sec:outlook}

This thesis has comprehensively examined the strengths and weaknesses of the methods. Based on the research conducted, new research objectives and questions have emerged.

A key aspect highlighted in this thesis is the interdependence of the reconstruction tasks in the dilepton channel.

The analysis presented here demonstrates that the two tasks are inherently linked. Therefore, it is strongly recommended that tools such as $\nu^2$-Flows, which perform neutrino regression, be extended to include jet assignment. This recommendation is based on the observation that a transformer trained for neutrino regression develops an internal understanding of the assignment structure. Consequently, a tool should generally provide both functionalities. This approach would also enhance consistency between jet assignment and neutrino regression, which may be compromised when using separate tools.

An important open question concerns the propagation of the neutrino posterior to derived event variables. It was demonstrated that averaging over the posterior of event variables, rather than computing event variables from averaged momenta, can reduce biases and enhance resolution. Extending this to more sophisticated models of posterior distributions, such as $\nu^2$-Flows, is expected to significantly improve reconstruction performance.

Furthermore, incorporating the posterior of an event variable into a fit or uncertainty estimation may be beneficial. This approach would provide a more comprehensive description of the underdetermined neutrinos.

Furthermore, it is possible to incorporate the probability distribution of reconstructed variables directly into the loss function. By employing automatic differentiation to compute the Jacobian, the probability density of the neutrinos can be transformed into the probability density of the reconstructed variables. This enables optimization of the model with respect to the variables of primary interest, such as $m(t\bar{t})$, \chel, or \chan. Direct regression of these variables could also be explored. In scenarios where interpretability is less critical than the discriminative power of the variable, training a discriminator may be advantageous. For example, in the search for contributions from non-relativistic bound state effects, a discriminative neural network can be trained to distinguish events from both states, thereby providing one or more sensitive variables.

As the tool has demonstrated improvements in analyses involving dileptonic decays of top-quark pairs, it is anticipated to be utilized in future studies. The most prominent application remains the investigation of bound-state effects at the \ttbar production threshold. For cross-section measurements, a significant reduction in uncertainty is expected when using the MultiModalTransformer. To support its application in measurements by \atlas, the tool has been integrated into the central software for top-related analyses: TopCPToolkit~\cite{topcptoolkit}.

Combined with the increased statistics provided by \LHCruniii in the near future, substantial progress in understanding these effects is anticipated.
